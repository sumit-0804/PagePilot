# The French Revolution

## Causes

By the late 1780s France was close to bankruptcy, partly because of the cost of wars, including its support for the American War of Independence. French society was divided into three estates: the clergy formed the First Estate, the nobility the Second Estate, and everyone else, the large majority of the population, the Third Estate. The Third Estate paid most of the taxes while the clergy and nobles enjoyed many privileges. Poor harvests in 1788 drove up the price of bread and caused widespread hunger and anger.

## The Estates-General and the National Assembly

King Louis XVI called a meeting of the Estates-General in May 1789 to find new taxes, the first such meeting since 1614. The deputies of the Third Estate declared themselves the National Assembly in June 1789. When they found their meeting hall locked, they gathered on a nearby indoor tennis court and swore the Tennis Court Oath, promising not to separate until France had a constitution.

## The fall of the Bastille

On 14 July 1789, crowds in Paris stormed the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison that had become a symbol of royal tyranny. The crowd was looking mainly for gunpowder and weapons. The day is still celebrated as France's national holiday.

## Rights and the end of privilege

In August 1789 the National Assembly abolished feudal privileges and adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen. The Declaration stated that men are born and remain free and equal in rights, and it set out principles such as freedom of speech and equality before the law.

## The Republic and the Terror

France abolished its monarchy and became a republic in September 1792. Louis XVI was tried for treason and executed by guillotine in January 1793. The Committee of Public Safety, led in practice by Maximilien Robespierre, then ran the Reign of Terror, during which tens of thousands of suspected enemies of the Revolution were executed or died in prison. The Terror ended in July 1794 when Robespierre himself was arrested and executed.

## The rise of Napoleon

After the Terror, a five-member executive called the Directory governed France from 1795, but it was weak and unpopular. In November 1799 the general Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in a coup, an event often taken as the end of the Revolution. Napoleon later crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804.
