# Cell Biology Basics

## The cell theory

Cell theory has three main ideas: all living things are made of one or more cells, the cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living things, and all cells come from pre-existing cells. Robert Hooke first used the word "cell" in 1665 after looking at thin slices of cork under a simple microscope. Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann later proposed that plants and animals are made of cells, and Rudolf Virchow added the idea that new cells only arise from the division of existing cells.

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria and archaea, have no nucleus; their DNA sits in a region of the cytoplasm called the nucleoid. Eukaryotic cells, found in animals, plants, fungi and protists, keep their DNA inside a membrane-bound nucleus. Eukaryotic cells are usually much larger than prokaryotic cells and contain many membrane-bound organelles that divide the cell into compartments.

## Organelles and their jobs

Mitochondria produce most of the cell's ATP through cellular respiration, which is why they are often called the powerhouse of the cell. Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis, where messenger RNA is translated into chains of amino acids. The rough endoplasmic reticulum is studded with ribosomes and helps fold and transport proteins, while the smooth endoplasmic reticulum makes lipids and helps detoxify drugs. The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles for delivery to other parts of the cell or for secretion.

Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that break down worn-out organelles, food particles and invading bacteria. Chloroplasts, found in plant cells and algae, carry out photosynthesis and contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Plant cells also have a rigid cell wall made mainly of cellulose and a large central vacuole that stores water and helps keep the cell firm.

## The cell membrane

The cell membrane is built from a phospholipid bilayer: each phospholipid has a hydrophilic head that faces water and two hydrophobic tails that face inward. Proteins embedded in the bilayer act as channels, pumps and receptors. The membrane is selectively permeable, meaning it lets some substances cross while blocking others. The fluid mosaic model describes the membrane as a flexible layer in which proteins and lipids can move sideways.

## Moving substances across the membrane

Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration, and it needs no energy from the cell. Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient and requires energy in the form of ATP, as in the sodium-potassium pump that keeps more sodium outside the cell than inside.

## Cell division

Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells and is used for growth and repair. Its stages are prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase, usually followed by cytokinesis, when the cytoplasm divides. Meiosis is a different kind of division that produces four sex cells, each with half the number of chromosomes of the parent cell, which creates genetic variation.
